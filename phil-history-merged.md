# Philippine History - Spanish Period

## Age of Discovery & Early Expeditions

**Ferdinand Magellan (1519)**
Departed from Sanlucar de Barrameda, Spain with 5 ships: *Trinidad, San Antonio, Concepcion, Victoria, Santiago*. San Antonio deserted and returned to Spain. Victoria was the only ship to complete the entire journey, led by **Juan Sebastian Elcano**. Magellan went to Spain because King Manuel I of Portugal did not allow him, so he was allowed by King Charles I of Spain. Named the Philippines **"Archipelago de San Lazaro"** because March 16 was the feast day of St. Lazarus.

- March 16, 1521: Landed on Homonhon island — first European to reach the Philippines
- Rajah Humabon of Cebu embraced Christianity; enemy Lapu-Lapu refused
- Humabon wanted Magellan to kill Lapu-Lapu; Magellan wanted to convert him
- April 17, 1521: Battle of Mactan — Magellan killed by Lapu-Lapu's forces
- Out of 5 ships and 300+ men, only Victoria and 18 men returned to Spain (Sept. 6, 1522)
- First circumnavigation of the globe — proved the world was round

**Juan Sebastian Elcano** — Master of ship "Concepcion", captained "Victoria" back to Spain. Elcano took over after Magellan's death. 16 more months to return. Entire voyage took almost 3 years.

**Rajah Kulambo** — Rajah of Limasawa, blood compact with Magellan (March 28)

**March 31** — First Mass in the Philippines by Fr. Pedro de Valderrama

**Rajah Humabon** — Rajah of Cebu
- Blood compact with Magellan
- Baptized April 14, became Don Carlos Valderrama
- Wife: Hara Humamay (Juana)

## Later Expeditions

After Spain celebrated Elcano's return, King Charles I decided Spain should conquer the Philippines. Five subsequent expeditions were sent.

- **Garcia Jofre de Loaysa (1525)** — sole survivor Andres de Urdaneta
- **Juan Cabot (1526)**
- **Alvaro de Saavedra Ceron (1527)**
- **Ruy Lopez de Villalobos (1542)** — 5th expedition from Spain
  - Departed Navidad, Mexico Nov. 1, 1542
  - Reached Mindanao Feb. 2, 1543
  - Named Samar & Leyte as **Las Islas Filipinas**
  - Named Mindanao as **Cesarea Caroli**
  - Colony in Sarangani failed due to insufficient food supply
  - Fleet left and landed on Tidore in Moluccas, captured by Portuguese
  - Villalobos remembered for naming the country "Islas Filipinas" in honor of King Charles' son, Prince Philip (later King of Spain)

## Spanish Colonization

**Miguel Lopez de Legaspi (Feb. 13, 1565)**
- First Governor-General of the Philippines
- Named Cebu as **"Villa del Santisimo Nombre de Jesus"**
- Since Loaisa to Villalobos failed, King Charles I stopped sending colonizers
- Philip II succeeded throne in 1556, instructed Luis de Velasco (viceroy of Mexico) to prepare new expedition headed by Legaspi, accompanied by Andres de Urdaneta (survived Loaisa mission)
- Feb. 13, 1565: Expedition landed in Cebu
- Proceeded to Leyte, Camiguin, then Bohol
- Blood compact with **Datu Sikatuna** in Bohol — sign of friendship, obtained spices and gold
- April 27, 1565: Returned to Cebu, destroyed Raja Tupas's town, established settlement
- 2,100 men from Mexico arrived, built **Fuerza de San Pedro** — Spanish trading outpost and stronghold
- **Martin de Goiti** expedition of 300 men left Cebu for Manila, found Panay and Mindoro
- Goiti arrived Manila May 8, 1570 — welcomed by natives, formed alliance with **Rajah Suliman**
- Locals sensed true Spanish objectives — battle erupted between Suliman's troops and Spaniards
- Spaniards more heavily armed, conquered Manila
- Legaspi arrived to join Goiti, built alliances with Rajahs **Suliman, Lakandula and Matanda**
- 1571: Legaspi ordered construction of **Intramuros** — walled city, proclaimed seat of government and capital
- 1572: Legaspi died, buried at San Agustin Church in Intramuros
- 1574: Manila titled **"Insigne y Siempre Leal Ciudad de España"** by King Philip II

**Dagami Revolt (1567)** — First revolt against the Spaniards

**Pampanga** — First province in Luzon inaugurated by the Spaniards

**Guido de Lavazaris (Aug. 20, 1572)** — 2nd Gov.-Gen. of the PH

**Limahong** — Chinese pirate who tried to attack Manila but fails

## The Galleon Trade

When Spaniards came, ancestors already traded with China, Japan, Siam, India, Cambodia, Borneo and Moluccas. Spanish government continued these trade relations — Manila became center of commerce in the East. Spaniards closed Manila ports to all countries except Mexico. Thus, **Manila-Acapulco Trade** or **"Galleon Trade"** was born — government monopoly.

- Only two galleons used:
  - Acapulco → Manila: 500,000 pesos worth of goods, 120 days at sea
  - Manila → Acapulco: 250,000 pesos worth of goods, 90 days at sea
- Allowed modern, liberal ideas to enter — eventually inspired independence movement
- Spaniards so engrossed in profits, hardly had time to exploit natural resources

## Basco's Reforms

**Jose Basco y Vargas** — Tobacco Monopoly (1778)
- Instituted reforms to free economy from dependence on Chinese and Mexican trade
- Implemented "general economic plan" — aimed at making Philippines self-sufficient
- Established **"Economic Society of Friends of the Country"**
  - Gave incentives for planting cotton, spices, sugarcane
  - Encouraged miners to extract gold, silver, tin, copper
  - Rewarded investors for scientific discoveries

### Tobacco Monopoly (1781)
- Tobacco industry placed under government control
- Implemented in: Cagayan Valley, Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur, La Union, Isabela, Abra, Nueva Ecija, Marinduque
- Each province planted nothing but tobacco, sold harvest only to government at fixed price
- No other province allowed to plant tobacco
- Government exported tobacco to other countries and to cigarette factories in Manila

- Started by letters from Malong narrating defeat of Spaniards
- Declared himself **"King of Ilocos"**, captured and executed
- His son was later declared "Prince of Ilocos"

**Francisco de la Cuesta**

## The Opening of the Suez Canal (1869)

The Suez Canal, connecting the Red Sea and Mediterranean Sea, was inaugurated in 1869. Built by French engineer **Ferdinand de Lesseps**. By passing through the Canal, vessels between Barcelona and Manila no longer had to pass by the Cape of Good Hope. Travel time shortened from 3 months to 32 days.

- Trading became increasingly profitable
- More foreign merchants and businessmen came to the colony
- Brought progressive ideas
- Filipinos gained knowledge about the world and desire for freedom

## The Secularization Controversy

Two kinds of priests served the Catholic Church in the Philippines:
- **Regulars** — belonged to religious orders (Franciscans, Recollects, Dominicans, Augustinians). Main task: spread Christianity
- **Seculars** — did not belong to any religious order. Trained specifically to run parishes, under supervision of bishops

Conflict began when bishops insisted on visiting parishes run by regular priests. Regulars refused — said they were not under bishop's jurisdiction. Threatened to abandon parishes.

- 1774: Archbishop **Basilio Santa Justa** upheld diocese's authority, accepted resignations of regular priests, assigned seculars
- Royal decree Nov. 9, 1774: Secularization of all parishes — transfer from regular friars to secular priests
- Regulars resented move — considered Filipinos unfit for priesthood, cited brown skin, lack of education, inadequate experience
- Controversy intensified when Jesuits returned to Philippines (exiled due to policies Spanish authorities disliked)
- Issue took on racial slant — Spaniards favoring their own regular priests over Filipino priests

**Monsignor Pedro Pelaez** — ecclesiastical governor of Church, sided with Filipinos. Died in 1863 earthquake that destroyed Manila Cathedral. After his death, others fought for secularization:
- **Fathers Mariano Gomez, Jose Burgos and Jacinto Zamora**

## The Propaganda Movement

The **illustrados** led the Filipinos' quest for reforms. Because of their education and newly acquired wealth, they felt more confident about voicing out popular grievances. However, since the illustrados themselves were a result of changes the Spanish government had been slowly implementing, the group could not push very hard for reforms. The illustrados did not succeed; but from this group arose the **intelligentsia** — more systematic, used peaceful means called the Propaganda Movement.

**Jose Rizal, Graciano Lopez Jaena and Marcelo H. del Pilar** were its most prominent members.

### Goals
Members (propagandists/reformists) worked inside and outside the Philippines. Their objectives:
- Recognition of the Philippines as a province of Spain
- Equal status for both Filipinos and Spaniards
- Philippine representation in the Spanish Cortes
- Secularization of Philippine parishes
- Recognition of human rights

The Propaganda Movement never asked for Philippine independence — members believed once Spain realized the pitiful state of the country, Spaniards would implement changes.

### The Propagandists

Filipinos in Europe were much more active in seeking reforms than those in Manila. Three groups:
1. Filipinos exiled to Marianas Islands in 1872 (implicated in Cavite Mutiny), later went to Madrid/Barcelona
2. Illustrados from Philippines sent to Europe for education
3. Filipinos who fled country to avoid punishment or couldn't stand Spanish atrocities

**Lopez Jaena** — brilliant orator, wrote "Fray Botod," "Esperanza," "La Hija del Fraile" — all criticized abuses of Spanish friars

**Del Pilar** — excellent writer and speaker, put up newspaper **Diariong Tagalog** in 1882. Popular writings:
- "Caiingat Cayo" — pamphlet answering criticisms of Rizal's Noli Me Tangere
- "Dasalan at Tocsohan" — parody of prayer books
- "Ang Sampung Kautusan ng mga Prayle" — satirical take on Ten Commandments

**Jose Rizal** — great novelist of the Propaganda Movement. First Filipino to become famous for written works.
- Wrote "Sa Aking mga Kababata" at age 8
- **Noli Me Tangere** (The Social Cancer)
  - Dedicated to Inang Bayan
  - Funded by **Maximo Viola** (300 php)
  - Published in Berlin, Germany by Berliner Buchdruckerei Aktiengesellschaft, **May 29, 1887**
- **El Filibusterismo** (The Reign of Greed)
  - Started writing 1887, finished **March 21, 1891** in Biarritz, France
  - Published in Ghent, Belgium by F. Meyer van Loo press
- Rizal executed at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park) on **December 30, 1896**

**La Solidaridad** — Established **December 10, 1888**
- First EIC: Graciano Lopez Jaena
- President: Galicano Apacible
- Last EIC: Marcelo H. Del Pilar

**El Ilocano** — First newspaper of the country (1889-1896)

**Diariong Tagalog** — First native daily paper
- Founded by Marcelo H. Del Pilar
- Funded by Francisco Calvo y Munoz
- Had Rizal's essay *El Amor Patrio* with pen name **Laong Laan**

The writings of the Propaganda Movement inspired Andres Bonifacio and other radicals to establish the Katipunan and set the Philippine Revolution in place.

### Why It Failed
- Colonial government rejected all demands
- Spain had internal problems — couldn't heed Filipino petitions
- Friars at height of power, displayed arrogance, refused to listen
- Reformists couldn't maintain united front — belonged to upper middle class, had to safeguard wealth
- Personal differences and petty quarrels, lack of funds
- No strong charismatic leader emerged besides Rizal

## La Liga Filipina & Katipunan

**La Liga Filipina** — Founded by Rizal, **July 3, 1892**
- House of Doroteo Ongjunco, Ilaya St., Tondo

**Pre-Exile Officers:**
- President: Ambrosio Salvador
- Fiscal: Agustin de la Rosa
- Treasurer: Bonifacio Arevalo
- Secretary: Deodato Arellano (first supreme leader of Katipunan)

**Post-Exile Officers:**
- President: Domingo Franco
- Secretary-Treasurer: Deodato Arellano
- Fiscal: Isido Francisco
- Secretary: Apolinario Mabini
- EIC: Marcelo H. del Pilar

**Objectives:** Unite the whole country, protect and assist all members, fight violence and injustice, support education, study and implement reforms

La Liga Filipina had no intention of rising up in arms; but Spanish officials felt threatened. **July 6, 1892** — only 3 days after founding, Rizal secretly arrested. **July 7, 1892** — Rizal exiled to Dapitan by Gov.-Gen. **Eulogio Despujol**.

Membership active at first, then drifted apart. Rich members wanted to continue supporting Propaganda Movement; others lost hope for reforms. **Andres Bonifacio** believed the only way to achieve meaningful change was through bloody revolution.

---

**Katipunan (KKK)** — **Kataastaasan, Kagalangalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan**
- Founded **August 1892** in a house on Azcarraga street (now Claro M. Recto), Tondo, Manila
- Solemn rite of **sanduguan** (blood compact) — each member signed name with own blood
- **Triangle system** of recruitment — each original member recruits 2 unrelated new members
- Monthly contribution: 1 Real (~25 centavos) for funds

**Objectives:**
- Political: Completely separate Philippines from Spain after declaring independence
- Moral: Teach Filipinos good manners, cleanliness, hygiene, fine morals, guard against religious fanaticism
- Civic: Encourage Filipinos to help themselves and defend the poor oppressed

**Supreme Presidents:** Deodato Arellano, Ramon Basa, Andres Bonifacio

**Officers:**
- Fiscal: Ladislao Diwa, Andres Bonifacio, Emilio Jacinto, Pio Valenzuela
- Secretary of State: Teodoro Plata, Jose Turiano Santiago, Emilio Jacinto
- Secretary of War: Teodoro Plata
- Secretary of Interior: Agueda del Rosario
- Secretary of Justice: Briccio Pantas
- Secretary of Finance: Enrique Pacheco
- Treasurer: Valentin Diaz, Vicente Molina
- Financier: Dario Valino

**Passwords per level:**
- **Katipon** (lowest, white): Anak ng Bayan
- **Kawal** (soldier, green): GOMBURZA
- **Bayani** (leaders, green & red): Rizal

**Kalayaan** — Katipunan newspaper

**Key Figures:**
- **Gregoria de Jesus** — First woman in Katipunan, Lakambini, Aling Oriang
- **Trinidad Tecson** — Mother of Biak-na-Bato, first woman to enter blood compact
- **Melchora Aquino** — Tandang Sora, Ina ng Katipunan, Ina ng Balintawak
- **Julian Felipe** — Nickname "demente viejo"
- **Jocelynang Baliwag** — Kundiman of the Katipunan
- **Marangan na Dalit ng Katagalugan** — Anthem composed by Julio Nakpil

The "Kataastaasang Sanggunian" (supreme council) was the highest governing body. Headed by a supremo/president. Each province had "Sangguaniang Bayan" (Provincial Council), each town had "Sangguniang Balangay" (Popular Council).

### Jose Rizal and the Katipunan
- Rizal never became involved in Katipunan's organization, but Katipuneros looked up to him as leader
- Rizal's name used as password among highest-ranking members (bayani)
- **June 1896**: Bonifacio sent **Dr. Pio Valenzuela** to Dapitan to seek Rizal's advice on planned revolution
- Rizal said timing not right — people not ready, not enough weapons
- Suggested gaining support of wealthy and influential Filipinos first
- Recommended **Antonio Luna** as commander of armed forces (military tactics expertise)
- Valenzuela returned **June 26** — Bonifacio agreed fatal to fight without weapons, but couldn't stop revolution
- Ordered men to prepare for battle, store food and supplies
- Battle plans made with **Emilio Jacinto** — headquarters near seas or mountains for easy retreat

## The Philippine Revolution

**July 7, 1892** — Rizal exiled to Dapitan via orders of Eulogio Despujol

**September 23, 1892** — Juan Luna shot his wife Paz Pardo de Tavera

**Republic of Kakarong de Sili (1896)** — In Pandi, Bulacan
- Supreme Chief: Canuto Villanueva
- Brigadier General: Eusebio Roque "Maestrong Sebio"

### Katipunan Discovered (August 19, 1896)
Rumors of secret revolutionary society had long circulated. Big break came when **Teodoro Patiño** told his sister **Honoria** about Katipunan's existence. Patiño was a worker at Diario de Manila printing press. Honoria lived with nuns at a Mandaluyong orphanage.

Honoria told Mother Superior **Sor Teresa de Jesus**. Sor Teresa suggested they seek advice of **Father Mariano Gil**, parish priest of Tondo.

Father Mariano Gil — accompanied by Guardias Civiles — searched Diario de Manila, found evidence. Governor general informed. Printing press padlocked. Hundreds of suspected KKK members arrested. Katipuneros fled to Balintawak.

**Haring Bayan Katagalugan (1896)** — Formed after Katipunan was snitched on. Andres Bonifacio -> Macario Sakay.

### The Cry of Pugadlawin (August 23, 1896)
Various wings of Katipunan gathered at house of **Juan Ramos** in Pugadlawin. Ramos was son of **Melchora Aquino** (Tandang Sora), later acknowledged as Mother of the Katipunan.

Bonifacio asked men whether willing to fight to bitter end. Everyone shouted approval except **Teodoro Plata** (thought too soon). Heartened, Bonifacio asked them to tear **cedulas** (residence certificates) to pieces as sign of defiance. Men tore cedulas, shouting **"Mabuhay ang Pilipinas"** — known as the Cry of Pugadlawin.

### The Katipunan in Cavite
Cavite became center of Revolution. Katipuneros divided into:
- **Magdalo** (Kawit): headed by **Baldomero Aguinaldo** (brother of Emilio)
- **Magdiwang** (Noveleta): headed by **Gen. Mariano Alvarez**

Battles:
- **Aug. 31, 1896**: Aguinaldo overran Kawit; Alvarez attacked Noveleta
- Bacoor: Aguinaldo tried to intercept Spanish reinforcements from Manila — repulsed, retreated to Imus
- **Sept. 5, 1896**: At Imus, defeated Spanish under **Gen. Aguirre** — 100 Spaniards killed, 60 weapons confiscated
- Aguinaldo hailed as hero — Caviteños called him **"General Miong"** (no longer "Kapitan Miong")
- **Oct. 31, 1896**: Aguinaldo proclamation for independence
- **Dec. 13, 1896**: **Camilo de Polavieja** replaced Ramon Blanco as gov.-gen.

**October 3, 1896** — Rizal to Barcelona
**October 4, 1896** — Deported
**October 6, 1896** — Back in Manila

**December 30, 1896** — **Consummatum Est!** Rizal executed via Camilo Polavieja

### Andres Bonifacio's Execution (May 10, 1897)
Aguinaldo recognized as leader by Magdalo; Bonifacio recognized by Magdiwang.

- **Dec. 31, 1896**: Assembly at Imus to settle leadership — unsuccessful
- **March 22, 1897**: **Tejeros Convention** — elected officers of revolutionary government
  - Aguinaldo won as president
  - Bonifacio relegated to Director of the Interior
  - Bonifacio rejected elections, declared void
- Bonifacio formed **Naic Military Agreement** — essentially creating contending government
- Captured, stood trial, sentenced to death by War Council
- Aguinaldo initially commuted sentence to deportation
- Later reversed commutation under pressure from **Pio Del Pilar** and other officers
- **May 10, 1897**: Executed at foothills of **Mt. Buntis** by **Major Lazaro Makapagal** on orders of **Gen. Mariano Noriel**

### The Revolution Continues
Bonifacio's death didn't deter Filipinos. Spanish government doubled efforts to control Cavite (seat of Revolution).

- **April 27, 1897**: Gov.-Gen. **Primo de Rivera** replaced Polavieja
- Marched to Naic, Cavite to persuade Filipinos to surrender — rebels stood their ground
- Aguinaldo realized Cavite no longer safe — moved to Batangas, camp in Talisay
- Spanish pursued — retreated to Morong (June 10, 1897), then to **Biak-na-Bato**, Bulacan

### The Biak-na-Bato Republic (1897)
Aguinaldo established headquarters in Biak-na-Bato, Bulacan. News spread — revolutionaries in high spirits. **General Iianera** (Nueva Ecija) declared support.

**July 1897**: Established **Biak-na-Bato Republic**, issued proclamation with demands:
- Expulsion of friars and return of friar lands to Filipinos
- Representation of Philippines in Spanish Cortes
- Freedom of the press and of religion
- Abolition of government's power to banish Filipinos
- Equality for all before the law

**Nov. 1, 1897**: Charter signed (based on Cuban Constitution) by **Felix Ferrer** and **Isabelo Artacho**
- Established Supreme council as highest governing body
- Outlined basic human rights: freedom of religion, freedom of press, right to education
- **Emilio Aguinaldo** elected Supreme Council president
- **Mariano Trias** elected vice president

### The Pact of Biak-na-Bato (December 15, 1897)
**Pedro Paterno** — Spaniard born in Philippines, volunteered as negotiator between Aguinaldo and Gov. Primo de Rivera.

Leaders:
- Emilio Aguinaldo — President
- Mariano Trias — Vice President
- Antonio Montenegro — Secretary
- Baldomero Aguinaldo — Treasurer
- Emilio Riego de Dios

**Dec. 23, 1897**: Spanish Generals **Celestino Tejero** and **Ricardo Monet** arrived, became hostages.

Agreement: Spanish government will grant self-rule to Philippines in 3 years if Aguinaldo goes to exile and surrenders arms. In exchange, Aguinaldo receives **P800,000 (Mexican Pesos)** as remuneration and amnesty.

- **Dec. 27, 1897**: Aguinaldo left for Hong Kong after receiving partial payment of **P400,000**
- Some Filipino generals refused to surrender arms — didn't trust Spaniards
- **Jan. 23, 1898**: Te Deum sung

### The Biak-na-Bato Pact Fails
Filipinos and Spaniards didn't trust each other. Periodic clashes continued even after Aguinaldo's departure. Spanish didn't pay the entire agreed amount.

## The Spanish-American War

### The US Helps Cuba
Before Cry of Pugad Lawin, revolution broke out in **Cuba** — another Spanish colony rose against abuses. Became independent 1898 after 3 years, with US help. Americans supportive:
- First: US is free country, advocated democracy and freedom
- 2nd: US wanted to protect $50,000,000 economic interest in Cuba
- 3rd: Stories of Spanish maltreatment of Americans in Cuba angered US citizens
- Finally: Cuba near US — covered by **Monroe Doctrine**

### US Interest in the Philippines
**Theodore Roosevelt** (Assistant Secretary of Navy) wanted war with Spain to strengthen/expand US Navy.
- **Feb. 25, 1896**: Ordered Commodore **George Dewey** to make Hong Kong headquarters of American Asiatic Squadron
- Directed Dewey to attack Manila Bay and destroy Spanish fleet when hostilities broke out

### The Spanish-American War
Spain didn't relish American intervention. With PH and Cuban revolutions, couldn't afford another enemy — US had more advanced technology and weaponry.

**Feb. 15, 1898**: American warship **Maine** blown up in Havana harbor — 260 officers and crewmembers killed. Unproven Spanish involvement, but Americans called for war.

- **April 23, 1898**: Spain declared war on US
- **April 25, 1898**: US declared war on Spain
- **May 1, 1898**: US Navy under Commodore George Dewey crushed Spanish squadron in Manila Bay and naval base at Sangley Point, Cavite
- By **June 1898**: Americans controlled portions of Philippine islands
- **Treaty of Paris (Dec. 10, 1898)**: US acquired Guam, Puerto Rico, Philippines for **US$ 20 million**

### The Battle of Manila Bay (May 1, 1898)
George Dewey (Commodore, US Navy's Asiatic Squadron) waiting in Hong Kong. Received cable from Roosevelt stating war had begun.

- Sailed from Hong Kong on flagship **Olympia** with 6 heavily armed ships
- Entered Manila Bay at dawn, saw Spanish ships under **Gen. Patricio Montoya**
- Ordered men to fire — battle began **5:41 AM**
- By **12:30 PM**: Spaniards raising white flag in surrender
- Spanish ships outnumbered Americans, but American weapons far superior
- Spanish: 167 dead, 214 wounded. Americans: no ships destroyed, no soldiers killed or injured
- One of easiest victories in world history

### The Siege of Manila
By **June 1898**: General Emilio Aguinaldo captured whole of Luzon, ready to storm Manila with **Gregorio del Pilar, Artemio Recarte, Antonio Montenegro, Pantaleon Garcia** and others.

- "Manila" referred to walled city of **Intramuros**
- Aguinaldo's men surrounded Intramuros; secured Tondo, Sta. Cruz, San Juan, Caloocan
- Spaniards hoped for reinforcements — none came
- Aguinaldo convinced Spaniards would surrender in days — started planning declaration of Philippine independence

## The Philippine Revolutionary Government

### The Malolos Congress (1898)
**July 18, 1898**: Aguinaldo issued decree for election of delegates to revolutionary congress. Second decree (5 days later): Aguinaldo would appoint representatives (elections not practical). He appointed 50 delegates.

**Sept. 15, 1898**: Revolutionary Congress assembled at **Barasoain Church**, Malolos, Bulacan. Pasig Band played national anthem. Aguinaldo read speech, congressional elections held.

Important achievements:
1. **Sept. 29, 1898**: Ratified declaration of Philippine independence (Kawit, Cavite, June 12, 1898)
2. Law allowing Philippines to borrow P20 million from banks
3. Establishment of Universidad Literatura de Filipinas and other schools
4. Drafting of Philippine Constitution
5. Declaring war against US on June 12, 1899

### The Malolos Constitution
Committee headed by **Felipe Calderon**, aided by **Cayetano Arellano**. Drafted by representatives of Filipino people — **first republican constitution in Asia**. Inspired by constitutions of Mexico, Guatemala, Costa Rica, Brazil, Belgium, France.

After minor revisions (mainly due to objections of **Apolinario Mabini**), final draft presented to Aguinaldo. Established:
- Democratic, republican government with 3 branches: Executive, Legislative, Judicial
- Separation of church and state
- Executive powers: President with cabinet
- Judicial powers: Supreme Court and lower courts
- Chief Justice elected by legislature with concurrence of President and Cabinet

### The First Philippine Republic (January 21, 1899)
Inaugurated in Malolos, Bulacan. Aguinaldo took oath of office. Constitution read article by article, followed by military parade.

**Apolinario Mabini** elected as Prime Minister.

Cabinet:
- Teodoro Sandico — Interior
- Baldomero Aguinaldo — War
- Gen. Mariano Trias — Finance & War
- Apolinario Mabini — Foreign Affairs
- Gracio Gonzaga — Welfare
- Aguedo Velarde — Public Instruction
- Maximo Paterno — Public Works & Communication
- Leon María Guerrero — Agriculture, Trade & Commerce

### The Philippine National Anthem
**Julian Felipe** (composer from Cavite) commissioned to write instrumental march for independence ceremony. Original title: **"Marcha Filipina Magdalo"**, later changed to **"Marcha Nacional Filipina"**.

Lyrics added **August 1899** based on poem **"Filipinas"** by **Jose Palma**. Original lyrics in Spanish, then English (Flag Law abolished during American period), then translated to Tagalog — title changed to **"Lupang Hinirang"**.

## Filipino-American Hostilities (1899-1901)

Aguinaldo agreed to peace conference — lasted **January 9 to 29, 1899**. Ended without results (Americans biding time for reinforcements).

**Feb. 4, 1899**: Hostilities exploded in San Juan. American soldier **Robert Grayson** saw 4 armed Filipino men on **San Juan Del Monte Bridge**, ordered them to stop — they ignored him. Grayson fired, Filipinos fired back.

Next day: MacArthur ordered troops to openly engage. **Filipino-American War** began.
- From San Juan, Americans marched to Pasig and nearby areas
- Overran Guadalupe, Pateros, Marikina, Caloocan

**Gen. Antonio Luna** and men showed heroism — attacked Manila night of **Feb. 24, 1899**
- Burned American living quarters in Tondo and Binondo
- Reached as far as Azcarraga Street (now Claro M. Recto Ave.)
- Met by formidable American troops, forced to retreat to Polo, Bulacan

American reinforcements arrived:
- **Gen. Elwell Otis** attacked northern Manila
- **Gen. Henry Lawton** went south
- **Gen. Arthur MacArthur Jr.** marched to Malolos (PH Republic capital)
- Malolos taken **March 31, 1899** — Aguinaldo already moved HQ to San Fernando, Pampanga
- **Gen. Fredrick Funston** crossed Pampanga River **April 1899**, entered San Fernando
- **May 5, 1899**: Americans gained control of Pampanga
- Aguinaldo fled to San Isidro, Nueva Ecija

### The Death of Antonio Luna
**Gen. Antonio Luna** — best and most brilliant military strategist of PH Revolution. Brave, intelligent, well-educated, but fiery temper and strict disciplinarian. Harsh manner earned enemies who plotted to kill him.

**June 1899**: Luna at command post in Bayambang, Pangasinan. Received telegram (allegedly from Aguinaldo) instructing him to proceed to Aguinaldo's HQ in Cabanatuan, Nueva Ecija.

**June 5, 1899**: Luna arrived at convent in Cabanatuan town plaza — told Aguinaldo left for Tarlac. Angry, Luna went out — met and killed by **Captain Pedro Janolino** with Kawit, Cavite troops. Buried at nearby churchyard. Aguinaldo's role unclear, killers never charged.

### Aguinaldo Flees
Military strategies began failing with Luna's death. Generals disagreed, Filipinos losing battles.

**Nov. 13, 1899**: Aguinaldo fled to Calasiao, Pangasinan with wife, son, mother, sister, Cabinet members. Americans in hot pursuit — Aguinaldo eluded them.

Realized constant running put women at great disadvantage. **Dec. 25, 1899**: Surrendered them to Americans. Continued to Palanan, Isabela (mountainous, difficult to approach). Loyal men guarded all roads.

### Aguinaldo is Captured (March 23, 1901)
**Gen. Funston** plotted capture. **Night of March 6, 1901**: Boarded American warship **Vicksburg**, docked at Casiguran Bay **March 14**.

**March 23, 1901**: Funston group reached Aguinaldo's HQ in Palanan. **Macabebe Scouts** pretended to be sent by Luna, with American officials as prisoners. Aguinaldo unaware until **Tal Placido** of Macabebe Scouts embraced him. Americans declared arrest in name of US government.

Aguinaldo brought to Manila, presented to military **Gov.-Gen. Arthur MacArthur Jr.** (father of Gen. Douglas MacArthur) at Malacanang Palace.

**April 19, 1901**: Pledged allegiance to United States.

### The Philippine Revolution Ends
First to yield: **General Simon Ola** — surrendered to **Col. Harry Bandoltz** in Guinobatan, Albay, **Sept. 25, 1903**. Others followed.

## American Colonial Period

### Military Government
**Gen. Wesley Merritt** — highest-ranking American official after Spaniards surrendered Manila **Aug. 13, 1898**. Established military government, became first American Military governor.
- Objectives: 1) Establish peace and order, 2) Prepare Philippines for civil governance

### The Schurman Commission (1899)
Chaired by **Dr. Jacob G. Schurman** (president of Cornell University). Arrived **Feb. 4, 1899**. Proposed:
1. Civil governments in areas with peace and order
2. Bicameral legislature — lower house all elective
3. American and Filipino members of upper house to head cabinet
4. Preserve Philippine natural resources
5. Create civil service system
6. Assign qualified Filipinos to important positions

US Congress adopted all recommendations.

### The Taft Commission (1900)
**March 16, 1900**: President McKinley appointed **Judge William Howard Taft** to head second Philippine Commission (Taft Commission). Taft would become Governor-General and later US President.

McKinley wanted to hasten transition from military to civil government. Taft Commission given executive and legislative powers.

- Arrived **June 3, 1900**
- First law: P2 million for roads and bridges
- **Sept. 1900 - Aug. 1902**: Enacted 440 pieces of legislation
  - Municipal and Provincial codes
  - Organized Philippine Constabulary and judicial system
  - Visited provinces for peace efforts

### The Spooner Amendment
Sponsored by **Sen. John C. Spooner** — allowed US president to fully administer Philippines. Military government replaced with civil (temporary pending permanent colonial government legislation).

### Civil Government under Taft (1901)
Inaugurated **July 4, 1901**, with **William Howard Taft** as first governor. Taft Commission continued as legislative body.

- **Cayetano Arellano** — first Filipino to hold high position: Chief Justice of Supreme Court (**May 28, 1899**)
- **Gregorio Araneta** — Secretary of Justice and Finance
- **Trinidad Pardo de Tavera, Benito Legarda, Jose Luzuriaga** — Philippine Commission members

### The Philippine Bill of 1902 (Cooper Act)
Sponsored by **Rep. Henry Allen Cooper**. Proposed creation and administration of civil government. Signed by President Theodore Roosevelt **July 2, 1902**.

Key provisions:
- Ratification of all changes by US president (Philippine Commission, civil governor, Supreme Court)
- Extension of American Bill of Rights to Filipinos (except trial by jury)
- Bicameral legislature: Philippine Commission (upper), Philippine Assembly (lower, elective)
- Executive powers of civil governor retained
- Philippine Commission as legislating authority for non-Christian tribes
- Judicial powers of Supreme Court retained
- Two Filipino resident commissioners to US Congress (no voting rights)
- Conservation of Philippine natural resources

Three provisions to fulfill before Philippine Assembly:
1. Complete restoration of peace and order
2. Nationwide census
3. Two years of peace after census publication

### The Philippine Assembly (1907)
Inaugurated **Oct. 16, 1907** at Manila Grand Opera House. **William Howard Taft** (US Secretary of War) as guest of honor.

- **Sergio Osmeña** — elected Speaker
- **Manuel Quezon** — elected Majority Floor Leader
- Assembly as lower house; Philippine Commission as upper house

### Resident Commissioners
First: **Benito Legarda** and **Pablo Ocampo**
Others: Manuel Quezon, Jaime de Veyra, Teodoro Yangco, Isaro Gabaldon, Camilo Osias

### The Jones Law (1916)
Enacted **Aug. 29, 1916** — first official document clearly promising Philippine independence as soon as stable government established. Replaced Philippine Bill of 1902.

- Created executive powers: Vice Governor-General with Cabinet
- Vice Governor concurrent as Secretary of Education

### The Creation of the Council of State
Upon recommendation of Quezon and Osmeña, Gov.-Gen. **Francis Burton Harrison** issued executive order **Oct. 16, 1918** creating first Council of State.
- Duty: Advise governor general on policy creation
- Met weekly or when called
- Composed of: Governor General, department secretaries, speaker of Lower House, Senate president

### The Os-Rox Mission (1931)
Delegation headed by **Sergio Osmeña** and **Manuel Roxas**. Went to US 1931 — influenced Congress to pass pro-independence bill by **Rep. Butler Hare, Sen. Henry Hawes, Sen. Bronson Cutting**.

**Hare-Hawes-Cutting Law**: 10-year transition period before US would recognize PH independence. President Hoover didn't sign; both Houses ratified.

Rejected by Philippine Legislature — specifically provision giving US president right to maintain military bases. **Manuel Quezon** tasked to head new independence mission.

### The Tydings-McDuffie Law (1934)
**Dec. 1933**: Quezon returned with amended version authored by **Sen. Millard Tydings** and **Rep. McDuffie**. President **Franklin Delano Roosevelt** signed **March 24, 1934**.

**Tydings-McDuffie Act** (officially Philippine Independence Act, Public Law 73-127): Established Commonwealth government for 10 years preparatory to granting independence.

## Commonwealth Period (1935-1946)

10-year transitional period under Tydings-McDuffie Law. Interrupted when Japanese occupied Philippines **Jan. 2, 1942**.

- Commonwealth government led by **Manuel L. Quezon** and **Sergio S. Osmeña** went into exile in US
- Quezon died of tuberculosis in exile; Osmeña took over
- Japanese installed puppet government in Manila — **Jose P. Laurel** as president (**Second Philippine Republic**)
- **Oct. 20, 1944**: Allied forces under **Gen. Douglas MacArthur** landed in Leyte
- Japan formally surrendered **Sept. 2, 1945**

After liberation, Commonwealth restored. Congress first regular session **July 9, 1945**.
- **Manuel Roxas** — Senate President
- **Elpidio Quirino** — President Pro Tempore
- **Jose Zulueta** — Speaker of the House
- First law: **Commonwealth Act 672** — organized Central Bank of Philippines

Collaboration issue: Counter Intelligence Corps presented accused collaborators. "People's Court" created.

**April 23, 1946**: Third commonwealth elections. **Sergio Osmeña** vs **Manuel Roxas**. Roxas won — last president of Philippine Commonwealth.

**July 4, 1946**: US granted independence as scheduled.

**Philippine Bill of 1902 (Cooper Act)** — See above

**The Philippine Assembly (1907)** — See above

**Resident Commissioners** — See above

**Jones Law (1916)** — See above

**Council of State (1918)** — See above

## Japanese Occupation of the Philippines (1942-1945)

### During World War II
**Dec. 8, 1941**: Japan invaded Philippines. Clark Air Base (Pampanga) attacked first, then Nichols Field (outside Manila).

**Dec. 22, 1941**: Japanese forces landed at Lingayen Gulf, continued to Manila. MacArthur declared Manila open city on Quezon's advice to avoid destruction.

**Jan. 2, 1942**: Manila occupied by Japanese. MacArthur retreated to Bataan; Commonwealth government withdrew to Corregidor, then to US.

**April 9, 1942**: Joint American-Filipino soldiers in Bataan surrendered. MacArthur escaped to Corregidor, proceeded to Australia.

**Death March**: 76,000 captured soldiers forced to march 100+ km to prison camp. Estimated 10,000 died from thirst, hunger, exhaustion.

### The Huks
Farmers of Pampanga banded together for protection. **Luis Taruc, Juan Feleo, Castro Alejandrino** and other leaders held meeting **Feb. 1942** in Cabiao, Nueva Ecija — agreed to fight Japanese as unified guerrilla army.

Next month: representatives from Tarlac, Pampanga, Nueva Ecija formed organization called **"Hukbo ng Bayan Laban sa mga Hapon"** or **HUKBALAHAP**. Taruc chosen leader, Alejandrino as right hand man. Members simply known as **Huks**.

### The Philippine Executive Commission
Per Quezon's instructions to **Jorge Vargas**, Filipino officials in Manila entered agreements with Japanese to mitigate people's suffering.

**Jan. 23, 1942**: Philippine Executive Commission established, Vargas as chairman.
- Benigno Aquino Sr. — Interior
- Antonio de las Alas — Finance
- Jose P. Laurel — Justice
- Claro M. Recto — Education, Health, Public Welfare
- Quintin Paredes — Public Works and Communication
- Jose Yulo — Chief Justice of Supreme Court

Next month: Election for **Preparatory Commission for Philippine Independence (PCPI)** — purpose: draw up constitution for free Philippines. **Jose Laurel** became head.

**July 10, 1943**: New Constitution finalized (against will of PCPI delegates). Ratified by **KALIBAPI** (*Kapisanan sa Paglilingkod sa Bagong Pilipinas*) — only political party allowed.

Constitution: 12 articles from 1935 constitution, noticeably lacked bill of rights, fitted Japanese wishes. Temporary — meant for chaos period only.

### The Second Republic (October 14, 1943)
**Sept. 20, 1943**: KALIBAPI under **Benigno Aquino Sr.** held convention to elect 54 National Assembly members (Assembly had 108 total — half were incumbent governors and city mayors).

**Jose P. Laurel** elected president of Second Republic. **Benigno Aquino Sr.** and **Ramon Avancena** as vice-presidents.

Inaugurated **Oct. 14, 1943** at legislative building steps in Manila. Philippine flag hoisted, national anthem played.

Japanese propaganda: giant posters, materials with slogans like "the Philippines belong to the Filipinos." Used newspapers, movies. KALIBAPI's main objective was promoting Japanese propaganda — but failed to gain Filipino trust.

### Gen. Douglas MacArthur Returns (October 20, 1944)
From Australia, Allied forces advanced toward Philippines, bombing Japanese strongholds.

**Sept. 21, 1944**: Bombings began. **Oct. 20, 1944**: Americans landed triumphantly in Leyte. MacArthur said: **"I have Returned."**

**Sergio Osmeña** part of MacArthur's group — had taken over after Quezon died at Saranac Lake, New York, **August 1944**.

**Oct. 23-26, 1944**: **Battle of Leyte Gulf** — biggest naval battle in world history. Almost destroyed entire Japanese fleet. US victory signaled beginning of liberation.

- Mid-December: Americans reached Mindoro
- **Jan. 9, 1945**: US forces docked at Lingayen Gulf
- **Lt. Gen. Tomoyuki Yamashita** (Tiger of Malaya) mobilized kamikazes — failed to stop Americans
- MAKAPILI units deployed — neither succeeded
- **Dec. 8, 1944**: Laurel and cabinet moved to Baguio on Yamashita's orders
- Japanese retreated to **Yamashita line** — jungle battlefront along Sierra Madre Mountains (Antipolo, Rizal to Aparri, Cagayan)
- Manila Japanese fought 3 weeks — surrendered **Feb. 23**
- **July 4, 1945**: MacArthur proclaimed general freedom from Japanese

## Philippine Independence from the Americans (July 4, 1946)

Filipinos fought countless battles for independence since 19th century under Spanish rule. 1896: Philippine Revolution started — Rizal executed, Katipunan organized in Cavite.

During Spanish-American War, Filipino leaders saw opportunity to free Philippines. Supported US with military forces. America summoned Aguinaldo from exile. With confidence in US relations, Aguinaldo anticipated independence.

**June 12, 1898**: Aguinaldo declared Philippine Independence from Spanish colonial government under First Philippine Republic. Philippine flag hoisted at 4:20 PM at Aguinaldo's balcony, Kawit, Cavite.

However, Admiral George Dewey convinced Spaniards to surrender to America — act of betrayal. America annexed Spanish colonies including Philippines. Filipino forces continued against imperialist power.

**Feb. 4, 1899**: Philippine-American War erupted. Filipino forces applied conventional then guerrilla tactics under Aguinaldo. Although Aguinaldo captured 1901, insurgencies continued (especially Muslim Moros in south).

America prepared Philippines for independence — civil government created. President Woodrow Wilson promised independence, entrusted authority to Filipino leaders.

**1935**: Philippine Commonwealth instituted under Tydings-McDuffie Act — self-government, though legislative power required US President approval.

Japanese invasion interrupted. Bataan surrendered; Quezon and Osmeña fled to America. WWII caused ~1 million casualties.

**April 1946**: **Manuel Roxas** elected President for independent Second Republic. American flag lowered in Luneta, Manila — Filipino flag raised. Independence granted **July 4, 1946**.

**1961**: President **Diosdado Macapagal** believed June 12, 1898 declaration brings memory of revolutionary heroes. Changed independence celebration from July 4 to **June 12** — celebrated to this day.

## The Philippines During Martial Law (1972-1986)

### Proclamation of Martial Law (September 21, 1972)
President **Ferdinand E. Marcos** placed Philippines under Martial Law. **Proclamation 1081** suspended civil rights, imposed military authority.

Marcos defended declaration — needed extra powers to quell violence allegedly caused by communists. Emergency rule intended to eradicate rebellion roots, promote national development. Martial Law described as strategic approach to defend Constitution, protect welfare from Muslim rebel groups and Christian vigilantes. Not a military takeover — only option to resolve rebellion. Emergency rule planned to lead to **"New Society"**.

Justifying events:
- Re-establishment of **Communist Party of the Philippines (CPP)** in 1968
- **New People's Army** growing in Tarlac and other areas
- Alleged assassination attempt on **Juan Ponce Enrile** (Minister of Defense) — gave Marcos window
- Insurgency in south — clash between Muslims and Christians
- Muslims defending ancestral land against Christian migrants
- **Moro National Liberation Front (MNLF)** organized in Malaysia — pushed for Mindanao autonomy

Initially supported by most Filipinos — seen as solving massive corruption. Ceased clash between executive and legislative branches. Marcos implemented reforms targeting elite rivals but not supporters.

- 30,000 opposition figures detained (including **Sen. Benigno Aquino**), journalists, student/labor activists
- Army and Constabulary seized weapons, disbanded private armies
- Marcos took control of legislature, closed Philippine Congress
- Media closed or tightly controlled
- Allegedly funnelled millions through trusted supporters — **"crony capitalism"**

### Deterioration of Political and Economic Condition
Decline of support triggered more Filipinos taking arms. Urban poor communities organized by Philippine Ecumenical Council for Community — protest masses, prayer rallies. Human rights violations pushed Marcos to hold elections in 1978 and 1981.

Marcos won both — but extended term elicited extensive opposition. Social unrest peaked after **former Sen. Benigno Aquino** murdered. Thousands called for removal.

**1986**: Marcos held snap election — hoped to satisfy masses, only increased determination to end his rule. **Corazon Aquino** (widow of Benigno) seated as President, ousting Marcos from Malacanang Palace — ending 21 years of tyrant rule.

## EDSA People Power Revolution (1986)

**February 25, 1986** — significant national event. Bloodless revolution praised worldwide. True empowerment of democracy — ousting tyrant without violence. Prayers and rosaries were the only weapons.

**Epifanio de los Santos Avenue (EDSA)** — 54 km stretch where peaceful demonstration held. Power of people restored democratic Philippines, ending oppressive Marcos regime.

### Background
Long oppressed freedom and life-threatening abuses since Martial Law Proclamation 1972. Rampant assassination of opposition. Height of patience: **former Sen. Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Sr.** shot and killed at airport **August 21, 1983** upon return from US exile.

His death marked the day Filipinos learned to fight. Widow **Corazon Cojuangco-Aquino** showed strength and courage to claim back democracy.

### The Snap Election
Depressing economy intensified resentment. Marcos held snap presidential election **Feb. 7, 1986** — confronted with strong opposition: Corazon Aquino. Most corrupt and deceitful election in PH history — evident electoral fraud between COMELEC and NAMFREL counts.

### The Revolt
Final straw — cry for democracy, demand to oust Marcos.

**Defense Minister Juan Ponce Enrile** and **Armed Forces Vice-Chief Fidel V. Ramos** withdrew support, called for resignation. Barricaded Camp Crame and Camp Aguinaldo.

**Archbishop Jaime Cardinal Sin** along with priests and nuns called for support. **Radyo Veritas** aired Cardinal Sin's message — summoned thousands to march EDSA. Nuns knelt before tanks with rosaries.

With prayers, armed marine troops under Marcos withdrew. Celebrities expressed support.

**Morning of Feb. 25, 1986**: Corazon Aquino took presidential oath at **Club Filipino**, San Juan, administered by **Supreme Court Associate Justice Claudio Teehankee**. Proclaimed **11th President** — first lady president.

People rejoiced — EDSA People's Power Revolution success. Bloodless, peaceful. Renewed power of people, strengthened democracy, restored democratic institutions.
