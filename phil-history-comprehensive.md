## Ferdinand Magellan's Expedition (1519-1522)

**Background:**
- **Ferdinand Magellan** (Fernao de Magalhaes) was a Portuguese navigator who sought a western route to the Spice Islands (Moluccas)
- King Manuel I of Portugal rejected his proposal, so Magellan sought support from **King Charles I of Spain**
- Charles I agreed, hoping to find a westward route to the East and compete with Portugal's monopoly on the spice trade

**The Fleet:**
Departed from **Sanlucar de Barrameda, Spain** on **September 20, 1519** with 5 ships and approximately 270 men:

| Ship | Captain |
|------|---------|
| *Trinidad* | Ferdinand Magellan (flagship) |
| *San Antonio* | Juan de Cartagena |
| *Concepcion* | Gaspar de Quesada |
| *Victoria* | Luis de Mendoza |
| *Santiago* | João Serrão |

**Key Events:**
- **1520**: *Santiago* wrecked on the coast of Patagonia
- **1520**: *San Antonio* deserted and returned to Spain — its crew revealed the route, giving Portugal knowledge of the passage
- **November 28, 1520**: Fleet entered the strait now named **Strait of Magellan** after 38 days of navigation
- **March 16, 1521**: Magellan sighted the islands of Samar — first European to reach the Philippines
- Named the islands **"Archipelago de San Lazaro"** because March 16 was the feast day of St. Lazarus
- **March 28, 1521**: Blood compact (**Sanduguan**) with **Rajah Kulambo**, Rajah of Limasawa — symbol of friendship
- **March 31, 1521**: First Catholic Mass in the Philippines held on the shores of Limasawa, officiated by **Fr. Pedro de Valderrama**
- **April 7, 1521**: Fleet anchored at Cebu
- **April 14, 1521**: **Rajah Humabon** (Rajah of Cebu) and approximately 800 followers baptized as Christians; Humabon took the name **Don Carlos Valderrama**
- Humabon's wife **Hara Humamay (Juana)** was also baptized
- Humabon wanted Magellan to kill his enemy **Lapu-Lapu (Datu Lapulapu)** of Mactan; Magellan wanted to convert Lapu-Lapu instead

**The Battle of Mactan (April 27, 1521):**
- Magellan, along with 49 armed men, sailed to Mactan to confront Lapu-Lapu
- Lapu-Lapu had approximately 1,500 warriors
- Magellan ordered his men to fire at the houses to provoke the natives, then engaged in hand-to-hand combat
- Magellan was struck by a bamboo spear, then surrounded and killed by Lapu-Lapu's forces
- **Lapu-Lapu** is considered the first Filipino hero — he successfully resisted foreign colonization

**After Magellan's Death:**
- The surviving Spaniards burned the *Concepcion* because they lacked crew to sail it
- **Juan Sebastian Elcano** took command of the *Victoria* and began the return journey
- Only **Victoria** and **18 European crew members** (plus 4 "Indios") returned to Spain on **September 6, 1522**
- **First circumnavigation of the globe** — proved the world was round
- The entire voyage took almost 3 years

---

## Subsequent Expeditions (1525-1542)

After Spain celebrated Elcano's return, King Charles I decided Spain should conquer the Philippines. Five subsequent expeditions were sent:

### 1. Garcia Jofre de Loaysa (1525)
- Departed Spain **July 24, 1525** with 7 ships
- Reached the Philippines but most men died from starvation and disease
- **Andres de Urdaneta** — sole survivor of significance; later joined Legaspi's expedition

### 2. Sebastian Cabot (1526)
- Sent to find Elcano's route but failed to reach the Philippines

### 3. Alvaro de Saavedra Ceron (1527)
- Departed Navidad, Mexico in 1527
- Reached Mindanao in 1528
- Attempted to return to Mexico via different route but failed (winds pushed him back to the Moluccas)
- Died in the Moluccas

### 4. Ruy Lopez de Villalobos (1542-1546) — 5th expedition from Spain
- Departed **Navidad, Mexico November 1, 1542** with 6 ships and approximately 400 men
- Reached Mindanao **February 2, 1543**
- **Named Samar and Leyte as "Las Islas Filipinas"** (in honor of Prince Philip, later King Philip II of Spain)
- Named Mindanao as **"Cesarea Caroli"** (after King Charles I)
- Colony in Sarangani failed due to insufficient food supply and hostile natives
- Fleet sailed to Tidore in the Moluccas, where Villalobos and his men were captured by the Portuguese
- Villalobos died in 1546 in the Moluccas
- **Most lasting contribution**: gave the country its name — "Islas Filipinas"

## Miguel Lopez de Legaspi's Expedition (1565)

**Background:**
- King Charles I stopped sending colonizers after Loaysa to Villalobos failed
- Philip II succeeded the throne in **1556**
- Philip II instructed **Luis de Velasco** (Viceroy of Mexico) to prepare a new expedition

**The Expedition:**
- Headed by **Miguel Lopez de Legaspi**, a Basque nobleman from Mexico
- Accompanied by **Andres de Urdaneta** (survived the Loaysa mission)
- **February 13, 1565**: Expedition landed in **Cebu**
- Legaspi named Cebu as **"Villa del Santisimo Nombre de Jesus"** (Village of the Most Holy Name of Jesus)

**Key Events:**
- Proceeded to Leyte, Camiguin, then Bohol
- **Blood compact with Datu Sikatuna** in Bohol — sign of friendship; obtained spices and gold
- **April 27, 1565**: Returned to Cebu, destroyed **Raja Tupas's** town, established a settlement
- **2,100 men from Mexico arrived**, built **Fuerza de San Pedro** — Spanish trading outpost and stronghold

**Conquest of Manila:**
- **Martin de Goiti** led an expedition of 300 men from Cebu
- Found Panay and Mindoro
- Arrived in Manila **May 8, 1570** — initially welcomed by natives
- Formed alliance with **Rajah Suliman**
- Locals sensed true Spanish objectives — battle erupted
- Spaniards, more heavily armed, conquered Manila
- Legaspi arrived to join Goiti
- Built alliances with Rajahs **Suliman, Lakandula, and Matanda**

**1571**: Legaspi ordered construction of **Intramuros** — walled city; proclaimed seat of government and capital
- Manila became the capital of the Spanish East Indies
- **1572**: Legaspi died, buried at **San Agustin Church** in Intramuros
- **1574**: Manila titled **"Insigne y Siempre Leal Ciudad de Espana"** by King Philip II

## First Revolts Against the Spaniards

### Dagami Revolt (1567) — First Revolt
- First recorded revolt against Spanish rule
- Occurred in the Visayas region
- Natives resisted Spanish attempts to impose tribute and forced labor

### Tondo Conspiracy (1587-1588) — Revolt of the Lakans
- Plan to overthrow Spanish government
- Also called **Revolt of the Lakans**
- Led by **Agustin de Legazpi** along with **Martin Pangan**
- Involved local rulers (lakans) and datus who planned to assassinate the Governor-General
- Conspired with the Japanese (under captain Tayfusa)
- Discovered by Spanish authorities; conspirators arrested and executed

## Church & Early Religious Institutions

**Domingo de Salazar** — First Bishop of Manila (1579)
- Arrived in the Philippines in 1581
- Advocated for the rights of the indigenous people
- Attended the Third Provincial Council of Manila (1582)

**Miguel de Benavides** — Third Bishop of Manila
- Served as Bishop of Nuestra Segovia
- Founder of **UST (University of Santo Tomas)** — Colegio de Nuestra Senora del Santisimo Rosario (1611)
- UST originally founded with 1,500 pesos
- Co-authored **Doctrina Christiana (1593)** with Fray Juan Cobo or possibly Fray Juan de Plasencia
- The Doctrina Christiana was the first book printed in the Philippines

**Keng Yong** — Publisher of Doctrina Christiana

**Ignacio Santibanez** — First Archbishop of Archdiocese of Manila (1598)
- Died shortly after appointment

**Colegio de Santa Potenciana (1598)** — First school for girls in the Philippines

**Francisco de Tello de Guzman (1596)** — Reestablished the Real Audiencia of the Philippines
- The Real Audiencia was a judicial court established to administer justice

**Fray Biancas de San Jose & Tomas Pinpin** — Typographic printing in the Philippines
- **Tomas Pinpin** — First Filipino printer
- Librong Pagaaralan nang mga Tagalog nang Uicang Castilla — first book printed by a Filipino (Tomas Pinpin)

## The Galleon Trade (1565-1815)

When the Spaniards came, ancestors already traded with China, Japan, Siam, India, Cambodia, Borneo, and Moluccas. Spanish government continued these trade relations — Manila became center of commerce in the East.

**The Manila-Acapulco Trade (Galleon Trade):**
- Spaniards closed Manila ports to all countries except Mexico
- Government monopoly — only the government could participate in the trade
- **Only two galleons used:**
  - **Acapulco to Manila**: ~500,000 pesos worth of goods, approximately 120 days at sea
  - **Manila to Acapulco**: ~250,000 pesos worth of goods, approximately 90 days at sea
- **Goods from Asia to Mexico**: silk, porcelain, spices, ivory, lacquerware, pearls
- **Goods from Mexico to Manila**: silver, gold coins, wine, olive oil, cloth

**Effects of the Galleon Trade:**
- Allowed modern, liberal ideas to enter — eventually inspired independence movement
- Spaniards so engrossed in profits, hardly had time to exploit natural resources
- Enriched a small group of merchants (the Manila Galleon traders)
- Did not develop the Philippine economy — only served as a transit point
- Chinese merchants settled in Manila, becoming important in the economy
- The **Parian** (Chinese marketplace) was established outside Intramuros
- **1815**: Galleon trade ended with the last galleon, Magallanes

---

## Basco's Reforms (1778-1787)

**Jose Basco y Vargas** — Governor-General (1778-1787)
- Instituted reforms to free economy from dependence on Chinese and Mexican trade
- Implemented "general economic plan" — aimed at making Philippines self-sufficient

**Economic Society of Friends of the Country (Sociedad Economica de Amigos del Pais):**
- Gave incentives for planting cotton, spices, sugarcane
- Encouraged miners to extract gold, silver, tin, copper
- Rewarded investors for scientific discoveries
- First established in 1781

**Tobacco Monopoly (1781):**
- Tobacco industry placed under government control
- Implemented in: **Cagayan Valley, Ilocos Norte, Ilocos Sur, La Union, Isabela, Abra, Nueva Ecija, Marinduque**
- Each province planted nothing but tobacco, sold harvest only to government at fixed price
- No other province allowed to plant tobacco
- Government exported tobacco to other countries and to cigarette factories in Manila
- Created resentment among tobacco farmers who received low prices
- Would later cause the **Ilocos Rebellion (1807)**

## Complete Chronological List of Revolts

### 1. Dagami Revolt (1567)
- **Location**: Visayas
- **Significance**: First revolt against the Spaniards

### 2. Tondo Conspiracy (1587-1588)
- **Location**: Tondo, Manila
- **Leaders**: Agustin de Legazpi, Martin Pangan
- **Significance**: First organized plot to overthrow Spanish rule
- Conspirators involved local rulers (lakans) and Japanese allies

### 3. Magat Salamat's Revolt (1589)
- **Location**: Tondo, Manila
- **Leader**: Magat Salamat
- **Reason**: Resistance to Spanish tribute collection
- **Outcome**: Leaders arrested and executed

### 4. Dagohoy Rebellion (1744-1829) — Longest Revolt
- **Location**: Bohol
- **Leader**: **Francisco Dagohoy (Dagohoy)**
- **Duration**: 85 years — longest revolt in Philippine history
- **Cause**: Spanish priests refused proper burial for his brother **Sagarino**, who died in a duel with a Spanish official
- **Outcome**: Finally suppressed by Governor-General **Jose Maria de la Vega** in 1829
- Approximately 20,000 Boholanos killed during the revolt

### 5. Tamblot Revolt (1621-1622)
- **Location**: Bohol
- **Leader**: **Tamblot** (a native priest/babaylan)
- **Cause**: Resistance to Spanish attempts to abolish native religious practices
- **Significance**: Defended indigenous religion against Christianity
- **Outcome**: Defeated by Spanish forces

### 6. Sumoroy Revolt (1649-1650)
- **Location**: Palapag, Northern Samar
- **Leader**: **Agustin Sumoroy**
- **Cause**: **Polo y servicio** (forced labor) — people sent out to different places to work despite directive to only do polo in their hometowns
- **Outcome**: Sumoroy killed; revolt suppressed

### 7. Maniago Revolt (1660)
- **Location**: Pampanga
- **Leader**: **Francisco Maniago**
- **Cause**: Workers made to work 8 months under unfair conditions, not paid for work, rice sold at unfair prices
- **Outcome**: Gave way to Andres Malong to start a bloodier revolt in Pangasinan

### 8. Malong Revolt (1660)
- **Location**: Pangasinan
- **Leader**: **Andres Malong** (Maestro de campo of Binalatongan)
- **Cause**: Same reasons as Maniago — abusive Spaniards, forced labor
- **Outcome**: Succeeded temporarily; declared himself **"King of Pangasinan"**
- Eventually defeated by Spanish forces

### 9. Almazan Revolt (1661)
- **Location**: Ilocos
- **Leader**: **Don Pedro Almazan**
- **Cause**: Inspired by letters from Malong narrating defeat of Spaniards
- **Outcome**: Declared himself **"King of Ilocos"**, captured and executed
- His son was later declared "Prince of Ilocos"

### 10. Ilocos Rebellion (1807)
- **Location**: Ilocos Norte and Ilocos Sur
- **Leaders**: Various local leaders
- **Cause**: Tobacco Monopoly imposed by Basco; farmers forced to plant tobacco at low prices
- **Significance**: First major revolt against the tobacco monopoly

### 11. Apolinario Dela Cruz's Cofradia Revolt (1841)
- **Location**: Tayabas (Quezon), Laguna
- **Leader**: **Apolinario Dela Cruz (Hermano Pule)**
- **Organization**: **Cofradia de San Jose** (religious brotherhood)
- **Cause**: Resentment over Spanish friars' control; desire for religious equality
- **Outcome**: Captured **November 1, 1841**, executed 3 days later
- **Significance**: Showed religious dimension of Filipino resistance

### 12. Silang Revolt (1762-1763)
- **Location**: Ilocos region
- **Leaders**: **Diego Silang**, then **Gabriela Silang**
- **Context**: British occupation of Manila (1762-1764)
- **Cause**: Desire for independence from Spanish rule
- **Key Events**:
  - **December 14, 1762**: Diego declared independence of **Ilocandia**, naming state "Free Ilocos" with Vigan as capital
  - **May 28, 1763**: Diego assassinated by **Miguel Vicos** (paid by Spanish)
  - **Gabriela Silang** continued the struggle — became known as **"Joan of Arc of Ilocos"**
  - **September 10, 1763**: Gabriela captured and executed in Vigan
- **Significance**: First Filipino to declare independence from Spain

### 13. Lavarias Revolt (1762-1765)
- **Location**: Ilocos
- **Leader**: **Juan de la Cruz Lavarias**
- **Cause**: British occupation inspired resistance

### 14. Palaris Revolt (1762-1765)
- **Location**: Pangasinan
- **Leader**: **Juan Palaris**
- **Cause**: British occupation and desire for independence

### 15. Igorot Revolt (1601)
- **Location**: Cordillera
- **Cause**: Attempt to convert Cordillera people to Catholicism
- **Key Event**: Padre Esteban Marin killed by Igorots
- **Significance**: Resistance to Spanish religious conversion in the highlands

### 16. The Cavite Mutiny (1872)
- **Location**: Fort San Felipe, Cavite
- **Date**: **January 20, 1872**
- **Cause**: Abolition of polo (forced labor) privileges for Cavite workers; increased tribute
- **Participants**: Soldiers, workers at the Cavite Naval Yard, native soldiers
- **Outcome**: Spanish authorities used the mutiny as pretext to arrest and execute **GOMBURZA** (Fathers Gomez, Burgos, Zamora)
- **Significance**: Sparked the Propaganda Movement

### 17. Basi Revolt (1807-1815)
- **Location**: Ilocos
- **Cause**: Government monopoly on basi (sugarcane wine)
- Farmers prohibited from producing basi for their own use
- **Outcome**: Eventually suppressed

### 18. Samarnon Revolt (1841)
- **Location**: Northern Samar
- **Leader**: **Dionisio Magallanes**
- **Cause**: Forced labor (polo y servicio)

## British Occupation of the Philippines (1762-1764)

**Background:**
- The Philippines was caught up in the **Seven Years' War** (1756-1763)
- Great Britain and Prussia defeated France and Spain
- The British sought to capture Manila as a strategic base in the Pacific

**The Occupation:**
- **October 6, 1762**: British forces under **Brigadier General William Draper** and **Commodore Charles Cornish** attacked Manila
- **October 5, 1762**: Manila fell to the British
- **Dawsonne Drake** — First British Governor-General, commissioned by British East India Company
- British forces looted churches and government buildings
- Loot included: **Php 5 million** in silver, plus stolen church treasures

**Filipino Resistance:**
- **Simon de Anda y Salazar** — Appointed Governor-General by the Real Audiencia
- Established provisional government in **Bacolor, Pampanga**
- Organized Filipino forces to fight the British
- **Diego Silang** — British allied with him; he declared Ilocos independent
- Silang later assassinated; **Gabriela Silang** continued the fight

**The Treaty of Paris (1763):**
- Ended the Seven Years' War
- Great Britain and Prussia won over France and Spain
- Spain ceded Florida to Britain; Britain returned Manila to Spain
- Philippines returned to Spanish control in 1764

**Impact:**
- Exposed Spanish military weakness
- Inspired Filipino nationalism
- Filipinos realized they could govern themselves

**Viana Memorial of 1765** — Written by **Leandro de Viana**, advised King Charles III to abandon the colony
- Believed the Philippines was too costly to maintain
- King Charles III did not follow the advice

---

## The Opening of the Suez Canal (1869)

The Suez Canal, connecting the Red Sea and Mediterranean Sea, was inaugurated in **1869**. Built by French engineer **Ferdinand de Lesseps**.

**Impact on the Philippines:**
- By passing through the Canal, vessels between Barcelona and Manila no longer had to pass by the Cape of Good Hope
- Travel time shortened from **3 months to 32 days**
- Trading became increasingly profitable
- More foreign merchants and businessmen came to the colony
- Brought progressive ideas (liberalism, nationalism, secularism)
- Filipinos gained knowledge about the world and desire for freedom
- Filipinos studying in Europe came into contact with nationalist ideas
- Led directly to the Propaganda Movement

## The Secularization Controversy

**Two Kinds of Priests:**
- **Regulars** — belonged to religious orders (Franciscans, Recollects, Dominicans, Augustinians); main task: spread Christianity
- **Seculars** — did not belong to any religious order; trained specifically to run parishes, under supervision of bishops

**The Conflict:**
- Bishops insisted on visiting parishes run by regular priests
- Regulars refused — said they were not under bishop's jurisdiction
- Threatened to abandon parishes
- **1774**: Archbishop **Basilio Santa Justa** upheld diocese's authority, accepted resignations of regular priests, assigned seculars
- **Royal decree November 9, 1774**: Secularization of all parishes — transfer from regular friars to secular priests
- Regulars resented the move — considered Filipinos unfit for priesthood, cited brown skin, lack of education, inadequate experience
- Controversy intensified when Jesuits returned to Philippines (exiled due to policies Spanish authorities disliked)
- Issue took on racial slant — Spaniards favoring their own regular priests over Filipino priests

**Key Figure — Monsignor Pedro Pelaez:**
- Ecclesiastical governor of Church, sided with Filipinos
- Died in **1863 earthquake** that destroyed Manila Cathedral
- After his death, others fought for secularization:
  - **Fathers Mariano Gomez, Jose Burgos, and Jacinto Zamora** (GOMBURZA)

## The Cavite Mutiny (1872)

**Location**: Fort San Felipe, Cavite
**Date**: January 20, 1872

**Causes:**
- Abolition of **polo** (forced labor) privileges for Cavite workers
- Increased **tribute** (tax)
- Growing resentment against Spanish friars

**The Mutiny:**
- Workers at the Cavite Naval Yard, along with native soldiers, rose up
- Killed some Spanish officers
- Quickly suppressed by Spanish authorities

**The Trial and Execution of GOMBURZA:**
- Spanish authorities used the mutiny as pretext to arrest **Fathers Gomez, Burgos, and Zamora**
- **GOMBURZA** — Mariano Gomez, Jose Burgos, Jacinto Zamora
- Three martyr priests executed **February 17, 1872** by garrote
- Alleged complicity in the uprising — no real evidence
- **Francisco Zaldua** — First to be executed (a snitch/informer)
- **Rafael de Izquierdo y Gutierrez** — Governor-General who ordered GOMBURZA's execution

**Impact:**
- Death awakened strong feelings of anger and resentment among Filipinos
- Inspired the organization of the **Propaganda Movement**
- Became martyrs for Philippine nationalism
- **Jose Burgos** — most vocal advocate of secularization; born in the Philippines
- **Mariano Gomez** — oldest of the three; parish priest of Tondo
- **Jacinto Zamora** — assistant priest; little evidence of involvement

## The Propaganda Movement (1880s-1895)

**Background:**
- The **illustrados** (educated, wealthy Filipinos) led the Filipinos' quest for reforms
- Because of their education and newly acquired wealth, they felt more confident about voicing out popular grievances
- However, since the illustrados themselves were a result of changes the Spanish government had been slowly implementing, the group could not push very hard for reforms
- The illustrados did not succeed; but from this group arose the **intelligentsia** — more systematic, used peaceful means called the **Propaganda Movement**

**Most Prominent Members:**
- **Jose Rizal**
- **Graciano Lopez Jaena**
- **Marcelo H. del Pilar**

### Goals of the Propaganda Movement
Members (propagandists/reformists) worked inside and outside the Philippines:
1. Recognition of the Philippines as a province of Spain
2. Equal status for both Filipinos and Spaniards
3. Philippine representation in the Spanish Cortes
4. Secularization of Philippine parishes
5. Recognition of human rights

**Important**: The Propaganda Movement **never asked for Philippine independence** — members believed once Spain realized the pitiful state of the country, Spaniards would implement changes.

### The Three Major Propagandists

**1. Graciano Lopez Jaena (1856-1896):**
- Brilliant orator
- Wrote **"Fray Botod"** — satirical work criticizing abuses of Spanish friars
- Wrote **"Esperanza"** and **"La Hija del Fraile"** — all criticized abuses of Spanish friars
- First editor of **La Solidaridad**
- Known as the "Prince of Filipino Orators"

**2. Marcelo H. del Pilar (1850-1896):**
- Excellent writer and speaker
- Put up newspaper **Diariong Tagalog** in 1882 — first native daily paper
- Funded by **Francisco Calvo y Munoz**
- Had Rizal's essay El Amor Patrio with pen name **Laong Laan**
- Popular writings:
  - **"Caiingat Cayo"** — pamphlet answering criticisms of Rizal's Noli Me Tangere
  - **"Dasalan at Tocsohan"** — parody of prayer books
  - **"Ang Sampung Kautusan ng mga Prayle"** — satirical take on Ten Commandments
- Last editor of **La Solidaridad**
- Died in Barcelona, Spain on July 4, 1896

**3. Jose Rizal (1861-1896):**
- Great novelist of the Propaganda Movement
- First Filipino to become famous for written works
- Wrote **"Sa Aking mga Kababata"** at age 8
- **Noli Me Tangere** (The Social Cancer)
  - Dedicated to Inang Bayan (Motherland)
  - Funded by **Maximo Viola** (Php 300)
  - Published in Berlin, Germany by **Berliner Buchdruckerei Aktiengesellschaft**, **May 29, 1887**
- **El Filibusterismo** (The Reign of Greed)
  - Started writing 1887, finished **March 21, 1891** in Biarritz, France
  - Published in Ghent, Belgium by **F. Meyer van Loo** press
  - Funded partly by **Valentin Ventura**
- Rizal executed at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park) on **December 30, 1896**

### The Publications

**La Solidaridad** — Flagship newspaper of the Propaganda Movement
- Established **December 10, 1888** in Barcelona, Spain (later moved to Madrid)
- First editor-in-chief: **Graciano Lopez Jaena**
- President: **Galicano Apacible**
- Last editor-in-chief: **Marcelo H. Del Pilar**
- Purpose: Voice the aspirations of the Filipinos for reforms
- Published in Spanish
- Ceased publication on **November 15, 1895**

**El Ilocano** — First newspaper of the country (1889-1896)

**Diariong Tagalog** — First native daily paper
- Founded by **Marcelo H. Del Pilar**
- Funded by **Francisco Calvo y Munoz**
- Had Rizal's essay El Amor Patrio with pen name **Laong Laan**

### Why the Propaganda Movement Failed
1. Colonial government rejected all demands
2. Spain had internal problems — couldn't heed Filipino petitions
3. Friars at height of power, displayed arrogance, refused to listen
4. Reformists couldn't maintain united front — belonged to upper middle class, had to safeguard wealth
5. Personal differences and petty quarrels, lack of funds
6. No strong charismatic leader emerged besides Rizal
7. Rizal's arrest and exile weakened the movement

# PART V: LA LIGA FILIPINA & KATIPUNAN (1892-1896)

---

## La Liga Filipina (1892)

**Founded by Jose Rizal, July 3, 1892**
- Location: House of **Doroteo Ongjunco**, Ilaya St., Tondo, Manila

**Pre-Exile Officers:**
- President: **Ambrosio Salvador**
- Fiscal: **Agustin de la Rosa**
- Treasurer: **Bonifacio Arevalo**
- Secretary: **Deodato Arellano** (first supreme leader of Katipunan)

**Post-Exile Officers:**
- President: **Domingo Franco**
- Secretary-Treasurer: **Deodato Arellano**
- Fiscal: **Isido Francisco**
- Secretary: **Apolinario Mabini**
- Editor-in-Chief: **Marcelo H. del Pilar**

**Objectives:**
- Unite the whole country
- Protect and assist all members
- Fight violence and injustice
- Support education
- Study and implement reforms

**Rizal's Arrest:**
- La Liga Filipina had no intention of rising up in arms; but Spanish officials felt threatened
- **July 6, 1892** — only 3 days after founding, Rizal secretly arrested
- **July 7, 1892** — Rizal exiled to **Dapitan** by Governor-General **Eulogio Despujol**

**After Rizal's Exile:**
- Membership active at first, then drifted apart
- Rich members wanted to continue supporting Propaganda Movement
- Others lost hope for reforms
- **Andres Bonifacio** believed the only way to achieve meaningful change was through bloody revolution

---

## The Katipunan (KKK) — Kataastaasan, Kagalangalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan

**Founded August 1892** in a house on **Azcarraga Street** (now Claro M. Recto), Tondo, Manila

### Organization Structure
- **Solemn rite of sanduguan** (blood compact) — each member signed name with own blood
- **Triangle system** of recruitment — each original member recruits 2 unrelated new members
- Monthly contribution: **1 Real (~25 centavos)** for funds

### Objectives
1. **Political**: Completely separate Philippines from Spain after declaring independence
2. **Moral**: Teach Filipinos good manners, cleanliness, hygiene, fine morals, guard against religious fanaticism
3. **Civic**: Encourage Filipinos to help themselves and defend the poor oppressed

### Leadership

**Supreme Presidents (Supremo):**
1. **Deodato Arellano** — First Supremo
2. **Ramon Basa** — Second Supremo
3. **Andres Bonifacio** — Third Supremo (most famous)

**Officers:**
- Fiscal: Ladislao Diwa, Andres Bonifacio, Emilio Jacinto, Pio Valenzuela
- Secretary of State: Teodoro Plata, Jose Turiano Santiago, Emilio Jacinto
- Secretary of War: Teodoro Plata
- Secretary of the Interior: Agueda del Rosario
- Secretary of Justice: Briccio Pantas
- Secretary of Finance: Enrique Pacheco
- Treasurer: Valentin Diaz, Vicente Molina
- Financier: Dario Valino

### Membership Levels
- **Katipon** (lowest, white): Password — **"Anak ng Bayan"**
- **Kawal** (soldier, green): Password — **"GOMBURZA"**
- **Bayani** (leaders, green & red): Password — **"Rizal"**

### Publications
- **Kalayaan** — Katipunan newspaper

### Key Figures
- **Gregoria de Jesus** — First woman in Katipunan, Lakambini, Aling Oriang
- **Trinidad Tecson** — Mother of Biak-na-Bato, first woman to enter blood compact
- **Melchora Aquino (Tandang Sora)** — Ina ng Katipunan, Ina ng Balintawak
- **Julian Felipe** — Composer of the national anthem; nickname "demente viejo"
- **Jocelynang Baliwag** — Kundiman of the Katipunan
- **Marangan na Dalit ng Katagalugan** — Anthem composed by Julio Nakpil
- **Emilio Jacinto** — "Brains of the Katipunan"; wrote Kartilya ng Katipunan
- **Procopio Bonifacio** — Brother of Andres

### The Kartilya ng Katipunan
- Written by **Emilio Jacinto**
- Code of ethics and principles of the Katipunan
- Emphasized love of country, honor, and service to fellow Filipinos
- Became the moral guide of Katipuneros

### Hierarchy of the Katipunan
- **Kataastaasang Sanggunian** (Supreme Council) — highest governing body
- Headed by a **supremo/president**
- Each province had **Sangguniang Bayan** (Provincial Council)
- Each town had **Sangguniang Balangay** (Popular Council)

---

## Jose Rizal and the Katipunan

- Rizal never became involved in Katipunan's organization, but Katipuneros looked up to him as leader
- Rizal's name used as password among highest-ranking members (bayani)
- **June 1896**: Bonifacio sent **Dr. Pio Valenzuela** to Dapitan to seek Rizal's advice on planned revolution
- Rizal said timing not right — people not ready, not enough weapons
- Suggested gaining support of wealthy and influential Filipinos first
- Recommended **Antonio Luna** as commander of armed forces (military tactics expertise)
- Valenzuela returned **June 26** — Bonifacio agreed fatal to fight without weapons, but couldn't stop revolution
- Ordered men to prepare for battle, store food and supplies
- Battle plans made with **Emilio Jacinto** — headquarters near seas or mountains for easy retreat

# PART VI: THE PHILIPPINE REVOLUTION (1896-1898)

---

## The Discovery of the Katipunan (August 19, 1896)

Rumors of secret revolutionary society had long circulated. The big break came when **Teodoro Patino** told his sister **Honoria** about Katipunan's existence.

- Patino was a worker at **Diario de Manila** printing press
- Honoria lived with nuns at a **Mandaluyong orphanage**
- Honoria told Mother Superior **Sor Teresa de Jesus**
- Sor Teresa suggested they seek advice of **Father Mariano Gil**, parish priest of Tondo
- Father Mariano Gil — accompanied by Guardias Civiles — searched Diario de Manila, found evidence
- Governor-General informed; printing press padlocked
- Hundreds of suspected KKK members arrested
- Katipuneros fled to **Balintawak**

---

## The Cry of Pugadlawin (August 23, 1896)

- Various wings of Katipunan gathered at the house of **Juan Ramos** in Pugadlawin (now part of Quezon City)
- Ramos was son of **Melchora Aquino (Tandang Sora)**, later acknowledged as Mother of the Katipunan
- Bonifacio asked men whether willing to fight to bitter end
- Everyone shouted approval except **Teodoro Plata** (thought too soon)
- Heartened, Bonifacio asked them to tear **cedulas** (residence certificates) to pieces as sign of defiance
- Men tore cedulas, shouting **"Mabuhay ang Pilipinas!"**
- Known as the **Cry of Pugadlawin** (or Cry of Balintawak)

**Note**: There is historical debate about the exact location. Some sources say it occurred in **Balintawak, Caloocan**, others say **Pugadlawin**.

---

## Early Battles of the Revolution

### Republic of Kakarong de Sili (1896)
- **Location**: Pandi, Bulacan
- **Supreme Chief**: Canuto Villanueva
- **Brigadier General**: Eusebio Roque "Maestrong Sebio"
- One of the first revolutionary governments established

### The Katipunan in Cavite
Cavite became center of the Revolution. Katipuneros divided into two factions:
- **Magdalo** (Kawit): headed by **Baldomero Aguinaldo** (brother of Emilio)
- **Magdiwang** (Noveleta): headed by **Gen. Mariano Alvarez**

**Key Battles:**
- **August 31, 1896**: Aguinaldo overran Kawit; Alvarez attacked Noveleta
- **Bacoor**: Aguinaldo tried to intercept Spanish reinforcements from Manila — repulsed, retreated to Imus
- **September 5, 1896**: At Imus, defeated Spanish under **Gen. Aguirre** — 100 Spaniards killed, 60 weapons confiscated
- Aguinaldo hailed as hero — Cavitenos called him **"General Miong"** (no longer "Kapitan Miong")
- **October 31, 1896**: Aguinaldo proclamation for independence
- **December 13, 1896**: **Camilo de Polavieja** replaced Ramon Blanco as Governor-General

### Rizal's Final Journey
- **October 3, 1896**: Rizal to Barcelona
- **October 4, 1896**: Deported back to Manila
- **October 6, 1896**: Back in Manila, placed under arrest
- **November 3, 1896**: Rizal's trial began
- **December 26, 1896**: Found guilty of rebellion, sedition, and conspiracy
- **December 30, 1896**: **"Consummatum Est!"** — Rizal executed via firing squad at Bagumbayan (Luneta) by order of Governor-General **Camilo Polavieja**
- Rizal's last words: **"Consummatum Est"** (It is finished)
- Rizal's poem **"Mi Ultimo Adios"** (My Last Farewell) was hidden inside an alcohol cooking lamp

---

## Andres Bonifacio's Execution (May 10, 1897)

**The Leadership Crisis:**
- Aguinaldo recognized as leader by Magdalo; Bonifacio recognized by Magdiwang
- **December 31, 1896**: Assembly at Imus to settle leadership — unsuccessful
- **March 22, 1897**: **Tejeros Convention** — elected officers of revolutionary government
  - Aguinaldo won as **president**
  - Bonifacio relegated to **Director of the Interior**
  - Bonifacio rejected elections, declared them void
- Bonifacio formed **Naic Military Agreement** — essentially creating a contending government
- Captured, stood trial, sentenced to death by War Council
- Aguinaldo initially commuted sentence to deportation
- Later reversed commutation under pressure from **Pio Del Pilar** and other officers

**The Execution:**
- **May 10, 1897**: Executed at foothills of **Mt. Buntis** by **Major Lazaro Makapagal** on orders of **Gen. Mariano Noriel**
- Bonifacio and his brother **Procopio** were killed
- **Gregoria de Jesus** (Bonifacio's wife) survived

**Significance:**
- Bonifacio's death created a deep division in the revolution
- Many historians consider it a tragic mistake
- Bonifacio is considered the **"Father of the Philippine Revolution"**

---

## The Revolution Continues

Bonifacio's death didn't deter Filipinos. Spanish government doubled efforts to control Cavite (seat of Revolution).

- **April 27, 1897**: Governor-General **Primo de Rivera** replaced Polavieja
- Marched to Naic, Cavite to persuade Filipinos to surrender — rebels stood their ground
- Aguinaldo realized Cavite no longer safe — moved to Batangas, camp in Talisay
- Spanish pursued — retreated to Morong (**June 10, 1897**), then to **Biak-na-Bato, Bulacan**

---

## The Biak-na-Bato Republic (1897)

Aguinaldo established headquarters in **Biak-na-Bato, Bulacan**. News spread — revolutionaries in high spirits. **General Ianera** (Nueva Ecija) declared support.

**July 1897**: Established **Biak-na-Bato Republic**, issued proclamation with demands:
1. Expulsion of friars and return of friar lands to Filipinos
2. Representation of Philippines in Spanish Cortes
3. Freedom of the press and of religion
4. Abolition of government's power to banish Filipinos
5. Equality for all before the law

**November 1, 1897**: Charter signed (based on Cuban Constitution) by **Felix Ferrer** and **Isabelo Artacho**
- Established Supreme council as highest governing body
- Outlined basic human rights: freedom of religion, freedom of press, right to education
- **Emilio Aguinaldo** elected Supreme Council president
- **Mariano Trias** elected vice president

---

## The Pact of Biak-na-Bato (December 15, 1897)

**Pedro Paterno** — Spaniard born in Philippines, volunteered as negotiator between Aguinaldo and Governor Primo de Rivera.

**Filipino Leaders:**
- Emilio Aguinaldo — President
- Mariano Trias — Vice President
- Antonio Montenegro — Secretary
- Baldomero Aguinaldo — Treasurer
- Emilio Riego de Dios

**December 23, 1897**: Spanish Generals **Celestino Tejero** and **Ricardo Monet** arrived, became hostages.

**Terms of the Agreement:**
- Spanish government will grant self-rule to Philippines in 3 years if Aguinaldo goes to exile and surrenders arms
- In exchange, Aguinaldo receives **Php 800,000 (Mexican Pesos)** as remuneration and amnesty
- Amnesty for all Filipino revolutionaries

**Aftermath:**
- **December 27, 1897**: Aguinaldo left for Hong Kong after receiving partial payment of **Php 400,000**
- Some Filipino generals refused to surrender arms — didn't trust Spaniards
- **January 23, 1898**: Te Deum sung (church service celebrating peace)
- Filipinos and Spaniards didn't trust each other
- Periodic clashes continued even after Aguinaldo's departure
- Spanish didn't pay the entire agreed amount

---

# PART VII: THE SPANISH-AMERICAN WAR (1898)

---

## Background: US Interest in Cuba

Before the Cry of Pugadlawin, revolution broke out in **Cuba** — another Spanish colony rose against abuses. Became independent **1898** after 3 years, with US help.

**Americans supportive for several reasons:**
1. US is a free country, advocated democracy and freedom
2. US wanted to protect **$50,000,000** economic interest in Cuba
3. Stories of Spanish maltreatment of Americans in Cuba angered US citizens
4. Cuba near US — covered by **Monroe Doctrine**

## US Interest in the Philippines

**Theodore Roosevelt** (Assistant Secretary of Navy) wanted war with Spain to strengthen/expand US Navy.
- **February 25, 1896**: Ordered Commodore **George Dewey** to make Hong Kong headquarters of American Asiatic Squadron
- Directed Dewey to attack Manila Bay and destroy Spanish fleet when hostilities broke out

---

## The Spark: Sinking of the USS Maine

- **February 15, 1898**: American warship **Maine** blown up in Havana harbor — **260 officers and crewmembers killed**
- Unproven Spanish involvement, but Americans called for war
- **"Remember the Maine!"** became the battle cry

---

## The War Begins

- **April 23, 1898**: Spain declared war on US
- **April 25, 1898**: US declared war on Spain

---

## The Battle of Manila Bay (May 1, 1898)

**George Dewey** (Commodore, US Navy's Asiatic Squadron) waiting in Hong Kong. Received cable from Roosevelt stating war had begun.

**The Battle:**
- Sailed from Hong Kong on flagship **Olympia** with 6 heavily armed ships
- Entered Manila Bay at dawn, saw Spanish ships under **Gen. Patricio Montoya**
- Ordered men to fire — battle began **5:41 AM**
- By **12:30 PM**: Spaniards raising white flag in surrender
- Spanish ships outnumbered Americans, but American weapons far superior
- **Spanish losses**: 167 dead, 214 wounded
- **American losses**: No ships destroyed, no soldiers killed or injured
- One of easiest victories in world history

---

## The Siege of Manila

By **June 1898**: General Emilio Aguinaldo captured whole of Luzon, ready to storm Manila with **Gregorio del Pilar, Artemio Recarte, Antonio Montenegro, Pantaleon Garcia** and others.

- "Manila" referred to walled city of **Intramuros**
- Aguinaldo's men surrounded Intramuros; secured Tondo, Sta. Cruz, San Juan, Caloocan
- Spaniards hoped for reinforcements — none came
- Aguinaldo convinced Spaniards would surrender in days — started planning declaration of Philippine independence

**August 13, 1898**: Manila fell to American and Filipino forces — Spanish surrender
- General **Wesley Merritt** — highest-ranking American official after Spaniards surrendered Manila
- Americans established military government

---

## The Declaration of Philippine Independence (June 12, 1898)

- **June 12, 1898**: Aguinaldo declared Philippine Independence from Spanish colonial government
- Philippine flag hoisted at **4:20 PM** at Aguinaldo's balcony, **Kawit, Cavite**
- Philippine national anthem played (composed by **Julian Felipe**)
- **Independence Day** — celebrated to this day

---

## The Treaty of Paris (December 10, 1898)

- US acquired **Guam, Puerto Rico, Philippines** for **US$ 20 million**
- Spain ceded the Philippines to the United States
- Filipinos were not consulted
- **Filipino-American War** began shortly after

---

# PART VIII: THE PHILIPPINE REVOLUTIONARY GOVERNMENT (1898-1899)

---

## The Malolos Congress (1898)

**July 18, 1898**: Aguinaldo issued decree for election of delegates to revolutionary congress. Second decree (5 days later): Aguinaldo would appoint representatives (elections not practical). He appointed 50 delegates.

**September 15, 1898**: Revolutionary Congress assembled at **Barasoain Church**, Malolos, Bulacan. Pasig Band played national anthem. Aguinaldo read speech, congressional elections held.

**Important Achievements:**
1. **September 29, 1898**: Ratified declaration of Philippine independence (Kawit, Cavite, June 12, 1898)
2. Law allowing Philippines to borrow Php 20 million from banks
3. Establishment of **Universidad Literatura de Filipinas** and other schools
4. Drafting of Philippine Constitution
5. Declaring war against US on June 12, 1899

---

## The Malolos Constitution

**Committee headed by Felipe Calderon**, aided by **Cayetano Arellano**.

- Drafted by representatives of Filipino people — **first republican constitution in Asia**
- Inspired by constitutions of Mexico, Guatemala, Costa Rica, Brazil, Belgium, France
- After minor revisions (mainly due to objections of **Apolinario Mabini**), final draft presented to Aguinaldo

**Key Provisions:**
- Democratic, republican government with 3 branches: Executive, Legislative, Judicial
- Separation of church and state
- Executive powers: President with cabinet
- Judicial powers: Supreme Court and lower courts
- Chief Justice elected by legislature with concurrence of President and Cabinet
- Bill of rights guaranteed

---

## The First Philippine Republic (January 21, 1899)

Inaugurated in **Malolos, Bulacan**. Aguinaldo took oath of office. Constitution read article by article, followed by military parade.

**Apolinario Mabini** elected as Prime Minister.

**Cabinet Members:**
- Teodoro Sandico — Interior
- Baldomero Aguinaldo — War
- Gen. Mariano Trias — Finance & War
- Apolinario Mabini — Foreign Affairs
- Gracio Gonzaga — Welfare
- Aguedo Velarde — Public Instruction
- Maximo Paterno — Public Works & Communication
- Leon Maria Guerrero — Agriculture, Trade & Commerce

---

## The Philippine National Anthem

**Julian Felipe** (composer from Cavite) commissioned to write instrumental march for independence ceremony.
- Original title: **"Marcha Filipina Magdalo"**
- Later changed to **"Marcha Nacional Filipina"**

**Lyrics added August 1899** based on poem **"Filipinas"** by **Jose Palma**
- Original lyrics in Spanish, then English (Flag Law abolished during American period)
- Then translated to Tagalog — title changed to **"Lupang Hinirang"**

---

# PART IX: FILIPINO-AMERICAN HOSTILITIES (1899-1901)

---

## The Outbreak of War

- Aguinaldo agreed to peace conference — lasted **January 9 to 29, 1899**. Ended without results (Americans biding time for reinforcements).
- **February 4, 1899**: Hostilities exploded in **San Juan**. American soldier **Robert Grayson** saw 4 armed Filipino men on **San Juan Del Monte Bridge**, ordered them to stop — they ignored him. Grayson fired, Filipinos fired back.
- Next day: MacArthur ordered troops to openly engage. **Filipino-American War** began.

**American Military Campaign:**
- From San Juan, Americans marched to Pasig and nearby areas
- Overran Guadalupe, Pateros, Marikina, Caloocan
- **Gen. Antonio Luna** and men showed heroism — attacked Manila night of **February 24, 1899**
- Burned American living quarters in Tondo and Binondo
- Reached as far as Azcarraga Street (now Claro M. Recto Ave.)
- Met by formidable American troops, forced to retreat to Polo, Bulacan

**American Reinforcements Arrived:**
- **Gen. Elwell Otis** attacked northern Manila
- **Gen. Henry Lawton** went south
- **Gen. Arthur MacArthur Jr.** marched to Malolos (PH Republic capital)
- Malolos taken **March 31, 1899** — Aguinaldo already moved HQ to San Fernando, Pampanga
- **Gen. Fredrick Funston** crossed Pampanga River **April 1899**, entered San Fernando
- **May 5, 1899**: Americans gained control of Pampanga
- Aguinaldo fled to San Isidro, Nueva Ecija

---

## The Death of Antonio Luna (June 5, 1899)

**Gen. Antonio Luna** — best and most brilliant military strategist of Philippine Revolution. Brave, intelligent, well-educated, but fiery temper and strict disciplinarian. Harsh manner earned enemies who plotted to kill him.

**June 1899**: Luna at command post in Bayambang, Pangasinan. Received telegram (allegedly from Aguinaldo) instructing him to proceed to Aguinaldo's HQ in Cabanatuan, Nueva Ecija.

**June 5, 1899**: Luna arrived at convent in Cabanatuan town plaza — told Aguinaldo left for Tarlac. Angry, Luna went out — met and killed by **Captain Pedro Janolino** with Kawit, Cavite troops. Buried at nearby churchyard.

**Significance:**
- Luna's death was a major blow to the revolutionary cause
- Aguinaldo's role in the killing remains unclear
- The killers were never charged
- Military strategies began failing with Luna's death

---

## Aguinaldo Flees and is Captured

**November 13, 1899**: Aguinaldo fled to Calasiao, Pangasinan with wife, son, mother, sister, Cabinet members. Americans in hot pursuit — Aguinaldo eluded them.

Realized constant running put women at great disadvantage. **December 25, 1899**: Surrendered them to Americans. Continued to **Palanan, Isabela** (mountainous, difficult to approach). Loyal men guarded all roads.

### Aguinaldo's Capture (March 23, 1901)

**Gen. Funston** plotted capture. **Night of March 6, 1901**: Boarded American warship **Vicksburg**, docked at Casiguran Bay **March 14**.

**March 23, 1901**: Funston group reached Aguinaldo's HQ in Palanan. **Macabebe Scouts** pretended to be sent by Luna, with American officials as prisoners. Aguinaldo unaware until **Tal Placido** of Macabebe Scouts embraced him. Americans declared arrest in name of US government.

Aguinaldo brought to Manila, presented to military Governor-General **Arthur MacArthur Jr.** (father of Gen. Douglas MacArthur) at Malacanang Palace.

**April 19, 1901**: Pledged allegiance to United States.

---

## The Philippine Revolution Ends

- First to yield: **General Simon Ola** — surrendered to **Col. Harry Bandoltz** in Guinobatan, Albay, **September 25, 1903**
- Others followed
- Remnants of revolutionary forces in various parts of the country gradually surrendered
